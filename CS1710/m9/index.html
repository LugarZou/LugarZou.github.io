Rising insights · 2 / 6

There is no "breakout" tier — and 458 tracks take half of all streams.

If breakouts were a different kind of track, the number of tracks would fall off a cliff somewhere between thousands and millions of streams. It does not: from 10 thousand to 10 million, every tenfold rise in streams cuts the number of tracks by about the same factor, about 3×. Our 59,000-stream breakout line is a convention — the 90th percentile — not a gap in the data.

Rising insights · 4 / 6

Main message · 5 / 6

A single standout country × genre square is not a reliable scouting signal. Before investing, check whether its lead is a level you can repeat or a handful of runaway hits.

The heatmap and the chance ellipse agree on where R&B in Brazil is — at the top — and disagree on what that means. Seen alone, the heatmap reads it as the best market. Seen against chance and against the rest of Brazil, it is one end of a country whose squares swing further in both directions than anyone else's.

Solution · 6 / 6

Three checks before you invest in a square.

Pick any square. The checklist runs the three tests from this story on it. The stadium shows who earned its streams: 100 seats are 100% of the square's streams, coloured by the kind of track that earned them. Darkest seats belong to tracks stuck on the 20-million cap — the runaway hits.

About the data

Spotify Music Analytics 2015–2025 (Kaggle; a synthetic dataset): 85,000 tracks, 12 genres, 10 countries, 8 labels. Stream counts are capped at 20,000,000 — 458 tracks sit on the cap, so their real counts are unknown. A breakout is a track at or above the 90th percentile of streams (59,000). Squares are compared in standard errors from the overall mean; the chance regions are the 68% and 95% contours of a χ²(2) distribution, tilted because popularity and breakout are correlated (r = 0.58). In frame 3, "any difference" is a χ² test of homogeneity across a panel's groups; the grey band, the chance-only draws and the typical best-to-worst gap all assume every group has the overall rate (20,000 simulated draws); a single pair is tested with a two-proportion z-test, Bonferroni-corrected for the number of pairs in its panel. The claims here hold for this dataset, not for the music industry.
