Rising insights · 2 / 6

There is no "breakout" tier — and 458 tracks take half of all streams.

− Against the pick: A few huge hits can paint a whole square red.

If breakouts were a different kind of track, the number of tracks would fall off a cliff somewhere between thousands and millions of streams. It does not: from 10 thousand to 10 million, every tenfold rise in streams cuts the number of tracks by about the same factor, about 3×. Our 59,000-stream breakout line is a convention — the 90th percentile — not a gap in the data.

Rising insights · 3 / 6

No genre, market, label or year has better odds.

− Against the pick: Being R&B or being Brazilian does not raise the odds.

Whatever makes one square red, it is not the category.

Rising insights · 4 / 6

The unusual squares are mostly Brazilian — and they point both ways.

± Mixed: R&B in Brazil really is unusual (+), but Brazil's other squares are just as unusual in the other direction (−). Brazil is noisy, not strong.

Each dot is one of the 120 squares. Right means more popular than average, up means more breakouts. The dashed ellipse is the luck zone: luck alone keeps 95% of squares inside it.

Main message · 5 / 6

A single standout country × genre square is not a reliable scouting signal. Before investing, check whether its lead is a level you can repeat or a handful of runaway hits.

− Verdict: Do not invest on colour alone.

The two views show the same square and ask two questions. Left: how much more does it stream than its genre elsewhere? Right: could luck alone put it there? Both put R&B in Brazil at the top. But the right view also shows the rest of Brazil scattered just as far out on the low side.

So R&B in Brazil is not the best market. It is one end of the noisiest country.

About the data

Spotify Music Analytics 2015–2025 (Kaggle; a synthetic dataset): 85,000 tracks, 12 genres, 10 countries, 8 labels. Stream counts are capped at 20,000,000 — 458 tracks sit on the cap, so their real counts are unknown. A breakout is a track at or above the 90th percentile of streams (59,000). "Streams vs genre" is the gap in mean log10 streams between a square and its genre across all countries, shown as the ratio of typical (geometric-mean) streams. Squares are compared in standard errors from the overall mean; the "luck zone" is the 95% contour (the inner ellipse 68%) of a χ²(2) distribution on that scale, tilted because popularity and breakout are correlated (r = 0.58). "Distance from average" is the Mahalanobis D² (cut-off 5.99). "N× as far as luck" is a country's mean D² over its expected value of 2; the country check flags p < 0.005. A square is "carried by runaway hits" when its count of capped tracks is in the top 5% of a Poisson with the size-matched expectation. In frame 3, "any difference" is a χ² test of homogeneity across a panel's groups; the grey band, the chance-only draws and the typical best-to-worst gap all assume every group has the overall rate (20,000 simulated draws); a single pair is tested with a two-proportion z-test, Bonferroni-corrected for the number of pairs in its panel. The claims here hold for this dataset, not for the music industry.
