Two ways to wire the same three views together

Milestone 6 asks whether coordinating views actually helps our audience — label scouting analysts — investigate the data. Both prototypes combine the same three selected sketches and chase the same question, which no single view can answer on its own:

Which country × genre should a scout invest in, and is that cell's advantage a higher general level (repeatable) or a couple of runaway hits (not repeatable)?

Prototype A — discrete drill-down

The heatmap is the control. One click selects a cell, a country row, or a genre column; the chance ellipses and the stadium rebuild for exactly that subset.

Easy to read, one subset at a time — and no way to compare two selections side by side.

Prototype B — brushing and linking, plus a shared year filter

The ring is promoted to 120 country × genre points and becomes the control. Brushing a region selects a set of cells, and the heatmap shows where that set lives.

Answers questions about groups of cells — but the brush has to be explained, and standard-error axes are unfriendly to the audience.

Views used (from the four sketches selected in Milestone 4)

JD-1 · country × genre heatmap

Jialing, Q3 — locates opportunity. Colour is a cell's mean log-streams against its own genre's benchmark; the grey bar is how many tracks back that row.

JY-1 · chance ellipses

James, Q1 — tests it. Both axes are standard errors from the overall mean, and the ellipses are where chance alone puts a group: 68% and 95%.

JY-5 · stadium seat grid

James, Q10 — explains it. One seat is 1% of the group's total streams, split by which slice of tracks earned it. This is our novel visualisation.

JD-5 was left out. Follow-up performance by debut group has debut cohort on its x-axis, which does not line up with the country × genre selection axis the other views share, so linking it would break its meaning. Milestone 6 allows using a subset of the sketches.

Data. The synthetic Kaggle “Spotify Music Analytics Dataset 2015–2025”, 85,000 rows. Breakout = stream count at or above the global 90th percentile (59,000 streams); the ellipse tilt comes from ρ = 0.578, the track-level correlation between popularity and breakout. Aggregates are precomputed by build_m6_data.py.
