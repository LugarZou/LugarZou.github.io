Two ways to wire the same three views together

Milestone 6 asks whether coordinating views actually helps our audience — label scouting analysts — investigate the data. Both prototypes combine the same three selected sketches and chase the same question:

Which country × genre should a scout invest in, and is that cell's advantage a higher general level (repeatable) or a couple of runaway hits (not repeatable)?
Prototype A — discrete drill-down
The heatmap is the control. One click selects a cell, a country row, or a genre column; the noise ring and the stadium rebuild for exactly that subset. Easy to read, one subset at a time, no way to compare two selections.
Prototype B — brushing and linking, plus a shared year filter
The ring is promoted to 120 country × genre points and becomes the control. Brushing a region selects a set of cells, and the heatmap shows where that set lives. A shared year range drives both views.

Views used (from the four sketches selected in Milestone 4)

  • JD-1 country × genre heatmap (Jialing, Q3) — locates opportunity
  • JY-1 standard-error ring (James, Q1) — tests whether the edge beats chance
  • JY-5 stadium seat grid (James, Q10) — explains who earned the streams

JD-5 (follow-up performance by debut group) was left out: its x-axis is debut cohort, which does not line up with the country × genre selection axis, so linking it would break its meaning.

Data is the synthetic Kaggle "Spotify Music Analytics Dataset 2015–2025", 85,000 rows. Breakout = stream count at or above the global 90th percentile (59,000 streams). Aggregates are precomputed by build_m6_data.py.
